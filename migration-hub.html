Bypass physical infrastructure limits, frequency coordination rules, and local range coverage boundaries with nationwide Push-to-Talk over Cellular systems.

Push-to-Talk over Cellular

Updated August 2026 · 5 min read

Instant group communication has always been the goal. For decades, conventional two-way radios delivered that capability for construction teams, logistics fleets, public safety, and field service crews. As operations spread across cities and counties, however, the physical limits of radio systems — range, repeaters, licensing, and capital cost — become harder to justify.

Push-to-Talk over Cellular (PoC) solves many of those constraints by moving the same one-to-many, button-press experience onto the 4G/LTE and 5G networks that already blanket most of the country.

What Exactly Is PoC?

PoC keeps the familiar radio workflow — press a button and talk to an entire group instantly — but carries the voice traffic as data over cellular and Wi-Fi instead of dedicated radio frequencies. There is no need for your own towers, repeaters, or frequency licenses. Coverage simply follows the commercial cellular footprint.

Four Practical Advantages Driving the Shift

Coverage That Travels With the Team

Traditional radio range is measured in miles from the nearest repeater. PoC range is measured by cellular coverage. Crews can stay connected from Melbourne to Jacksonville, across the state, or even into neighboring states without adding infrastructure.

Predictable Operating Costs

Building a private radio system requires capital for hardware, site leases, and licenses, plus ongoing maintenance. PoC typically uses a monthly subscription model. Organizations convert a large upfront expense into a steady operational cost that scales with headcount.

Features Built for Modern Work

Professional PoC devices commonly include GPS location tracking, photo and form sharing, emergency Man-Down and Lone Worker alerts, and strong noise cancellation — tools that help dispatch, safety, and accountability without extra hardware.

Safer, Compliant Operation for Drivers

Many commercial drivers cannot legally hold a phone while the vehicle is moving. Purpose-built PoC radios with physical PTT buttons and external microphones keep communication hands-free and help fleets stay aligned with safety rules.

When Traditional Radio Still Makes Sense

Conventional two-way radios remain the better choice in deep rural or underground locations where cellular service is unreliable or nonexistent. They also require no monthly data plan and can operate completely independently of commercial networks. Many organizations therefore keep a hybrid approach: traditional radios for the local job site and PoC for wide-area coordination.

Who Benefits Most From PoC?

  • Logistics and distribution fleets that cross multiple counties or states
  • Field service companies (HVAC, electrical, plumbing) with crews spread across Central Florida
  • School districts and campus security teams covering several locations
  • Construction and infrastructure projects that move from site to site
  • Any operation that needs GPS visibility and group messaging in addition to voice
